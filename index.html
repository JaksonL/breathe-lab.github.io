Bioengineering for Respiratory Infections

We build human in vitro models — alveolus-on-chip, iPSC-derived lung cells and engineered tissues — to understand how Mycobacterium tuberculosis interacts with the lung, and why the lung so often fails to recover after TB is cured.

Our research Join or collaborate

New Approach Methodologies

Human-relevant, animal-free models of host–pathogen interaction, built on microfabrication and stem-cell engineering.

Post-TB lung disease

Mechanisms of fibrosis and impaired recovery after treatment, linking in vitro findings to patient cohorts in East London.

Translational focus

Embedded in the QMUL and Barts TB Centre with access to clinical cohorts, CL3 facilities and the Centre of Predictive in vitro Models.
